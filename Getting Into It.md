I use a bike as my main mode of transportation because it saves time walking between classes while still providing me with regular exercise. The amount of time lost by walking over biking adds up really quickly. Some of my friends who I've convinced to start biking have told me how much time they save now. My decision to build a lighter, new bike was spurred by two things: 1) my experiences trying to bike up the hill to the School of Business at UCR, and 2) the fact that I was about to transfer to UCLA, a remarkably hilly campus. My old bike is 29 pounds and, in my experience, is incredibly difficult to climb inclines. 

The environment and purpose of biking are important factors in what kind of bike to build (if you want an efficient bike). You wouldn't typically use a pocket knife to cut down a redwood tree. I needed a bike that would be comfortable climbing hills with a 15 lb backpack, responsive, durable, and capable of braking on a dime. During this project, I asked loads and loads of people (and the internet) for advice. 

The first person I asked was my uncle, who is a major bike enthusiast. He emphasized the importance of the frame, since it essentially defines what the bike can do and which components are compatible. Typically, the most important specifications are sizing, bike geometry, material, and generation. For me, I needed a 54cm bike, considering I am 5'9" with a 31.75" inseam. Given the rugged and less bike-friendly terrain, I decided that a gravel or cyclocross bike would be the best for me due to their steering capabilities and ability to equip wider tires for lower tire pressure.
